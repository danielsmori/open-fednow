# FedNow adoption context and integration questions

FedNow launched in July 2023. The Federal Reserve reported **more than 1,800 financial institutions live on the service in July 2026**; its participant list is updated separately and should be consulted for a current count. That network figure does not establish how many institutions need this software, or why any particular institution has or has not joined. [Federal Reserve participation update](https://www.frbservices.org/news/fed360/issues/soaring-volume-participation-three-years-fednow-service/) · [participant list](https://www.frbservices.org/financial-services/fednow/organizations/)

A bank evaluating instant payments may need to understand its core's availability, authoritative balance and reservation controls, status inquiry, message mapping, security, and recovery across failures. These are institution-specific engineering questions. FedNow operates continuously, but a local cached balance or provisional response is not proof that a participant can remain available safely during core maintenance. The [public operating procedures](https://www.frbservices.org/resources/rules-regulations/operating-circulars/) and credentialed technical materials, along with the institution's own core and risk controls, govern a live implementation.

OpenFedNow provides a synthetic environment for examining those questions. Its original five-layer design includes a Redis Shadow Ledger, gateway and vendor-shaped adapters. The newer [SQL reliability path](reliability/architecture.md) tests scoped idempotency, holds, ambiguous outcomes, inquiry and recovery with independent local effect observations. A guarded `/fednow/send` bridge connects that service to the existing entry point only for non-production evaluation. The [capability matrix](capability-matrix.md) separates these results from the disabled legacy outbound/return routes and unverified vendor capabilities.

The repository has **not** measured national adoption barriers, implementation cost or savings, market coverage, bank demand, or benefit to a particular institution. It has not validated an 87/13 engineering split, vendor market-share coverage, or a production deployment. Any such claim needs its own traceable source and methodology; synthetic tests alone cannot support it.
